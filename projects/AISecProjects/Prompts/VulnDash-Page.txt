1. BUSINESS OBJECTIVE
As an MSP/MSSP , we currently spend significant engineering time manually preparing and distributing emails whenever critical or zero-day vulnerabilities are disclosed.
We want to eliminate repetitive, non-actionable vulnerability notification emails by providing clients with a centralized Security Advisory Center.
The portal will provide general awareness and transparency regarding critical vulnerabilities, zero-days, and selected high-severity security advisories.
When a vulnerability is confirmed to affect a client's environment, our existing ServiceNow remediation and notification process will continue separately.
The new portal must not imply that every vulnerability has been assessed against every client environment.
Desired outcome
	•	Engineers create an advisory once.
	•	An authorized reviewer approves it.
	•	The approved advisory is automatically published to SharePoint.
	•	All authorized client guests can view published advisories.
	•	Clients can search, filter, and review advisory details.
	•	Engineers no longer need to distribute individual awareness emails for routine advisories.
	•	Direct notification remains available for confirmed client exposure, urgent action, or contractual requirements.

6. POWER AUTOMATE APPROVAL AND PUBLICATION
Build a Power Automate workflow named:
SEC-ADV-01 - Review and Publish Security Advisory
Required workflow:
	1	Engineer creates or updates an internal advisory.
	2	Engineer submits the advisory for review.
	3	Workflow sends approval to an authorized security reviewer.
	4	Reviewer approves or rejects.
	5	If rejected, the advisory returns to Draft with reviewer comments.
	6	If approved, required fields are validated.
	7	Workflow creates or updates the corresponding public advisory.
	8	Workflow records the publication timestamp and version.
	9	Internal publication status is updated only after successful publication.
	10	Publication failures are logged and reported to an internal administrator.
Critical technical requirements
	•	Prevent duplicate approval requests.
	•	Prevent infinite loops from SharePoint item updates.
	•	Use AdvisoryID for idempotent publication.
	•	Prevent concurrent publication races.
	•	Do not publish modified content that has not been approved.
	•	Recheck source version before publication.
	•	Require approval for material changes.
	•	Support advisory withdrawal and correction.
	•	Preserve an audit trail.
	•	Do not publish internal-only fields.
	•	Handle Power Automate connector errors and throttling.
	•	Use least-privilege service identities.
	•	Avoid hard-coded passwords, secrets, and tokens.
Use native Power Automate approval actions and SharePoint connector actions where supported.
If an automation cannot be deployed reliably through an API or script, produce the exact flow construction instructions and all required expressions, mappings, conditions, and configuration values.
Do not invent unsupported Power Automate actions, import formats, or deployment capabilities.
